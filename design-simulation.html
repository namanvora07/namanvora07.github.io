Lightweight Skateboard Design Optimization

01
CFRP–foam sandwich structure · topography & size optimization · VARTM fabrication · Clemson University
Altair HyperMesh OptiStruct SolidWorks DFMPro VARTM
CHALLENGE

Design and optimize a skateboard while minimizing mass, under a deflection constraint of less than 20 mm. Mass and stiffness pull directly against each other, so the design had to find load paths efficient enough to remove material without losing structural performance — and then survive real fabrication, not just simulation.

APPROACH
  • Modeled the board as a three-layer sandwich structure — woven carbon-fiber faces carrying bending stress, XPS foam core carrying shear.
  • Utilized Altair HyperMesh to run topography optimization to generate an efficient rib/load-path geometry, then size optimization on ply thickness to refine the result.
  • Translated the optimized geometry into a manufacturable two-part mold using DFMPro, adopting an inverted-print approach for FDM printing.
  • Fabricated the part by Vacuum-Assisted Resin Transfer Molding, then reinforced and re-infused after the first prototype under-performed against predictions.
OUTCOME

Delivered a final skateboard prototype at 364 g — a 33.4% mass reduction from the initial CAD design — with 4 mm of measured deflection under load, well inside the 20 mm limit. Topography optimization alone cut peak stress from 238 MPa to 94.8 MPa. The reinforced prototype was validated against a load of 1,200 N in simulation and 700 N in the real world, validating the design in hardware rather than in simulation alone.

Bio-Inspired Interlocking Joints for a Clamshell E-Bike Frame

02
Ironclad beetle–inspired joint geometry · ASTM D638 & D790 mechanical testing · Clemson University
Biomimetic Design Parametric CAD 3D Printing Mechanical Testing Adhesive Bonding
CHALLENGE

Design and develop a joining concept for a two-piece composite e-bike frame that simplifies manufacturing and assembly while maintaining effective load transfer. Investigate beetle-inspired interlocking features to resist joint separation and reduce the risk of abrupt failure.

APPROACH
  • Designed the interlock: Developed parametric elliptical features with a 25° contact angle, inspired by ironclad-beetle joint geometry, to mechanically engage the two mating halves.
  • Built and evaluated prototypes: Produced 3D-printed ABS specimens across multiple thicknesses and assembly configurations, documenting warpage and dimensional inaccuracies during prototype development.
  • Benchmarked mechanical performance: Compared solid references, sliding interlocks, and printed assemblies through tensile and flexural testing, using ASTM D638 and ASTM D790 as test references.
  • Evaluated adhesive reinforcement: Used J-B Plastic Bonder to compare flat and interlocking bonded interfaces against single-part controls, assessing peak loads and force–displacement behavior.
OUTCOME / RESULTS
  • Approximately 10.6× the peak tensile load: The adhesive-bonded interlocking specimen reached 2,564 N, compared with 243 N for the flat bonded specimen.
  • Approximately 46.4% higher flexural load-bearing capacity: The bonded interlocking specimen reached a peak of 1,148 N, outperforming the solid baseline specimens.

NASCAR Vehicle Aerodynamic Optimization

03
Greenhouse-angle drag reduction study · Deep Orange 18 · Clemson University
STAR-CCM+ CFD Aerodynamics
CHALLENGE

Reduce aerodynamic drag on a closed-wheel vehicle body without compromising the packaging and structural requirements of the platform.

APPROACH

Evaluated a series of body surface changes in STAR-CCM+, isolating greenhouse angle as the dominant variable and iterating on it to find the drag-optimal profile. The CFD simulation was carried out for a speed of 200 miles per hour, using a k-epsilon solver.

OUTCOME

Reduced the drag coefficient from 0.51 to 0.34 — a 33% improvement — through greenhouse-angle optimization.

EV Battery Thermal Management with Nanofluids

04
Nanofluid cooling performance study · 720-case CFD campaign · Simulation Labs
ANSYS Fluent Thermal Analysis Material Selection
CHALLENGE

EV battery packs need cooling that both removes heat quickly and holds temperature steady. Conventional coolants trade one against the other, so the question was which nanofluid — if any — performs best, and whether the best heat-transfer fluid is also the most thermally stable.

APPROACH

Led R&D evaluating nanofluid-based cooling concepts across a matrix of operating conditions — battery surface temperatures from 30–35 °C against thermoelectric-cooler temperatures from 2–18 °C. Simulated 12 nanofluid materials in ANSYS Fluent at 60 test cases each, for 720 cases total, comparing heat-transfer behavior against temperature stability.

OUTCOME

Identified copper and gold nanofluids as the strongest heat-transfer performers, with aluminium oxide and silver offering the best thermal stability — giving the team a clear, evidence-backed basis for material selection depending on whether a pack design prioritizes peak cooling rate or steady-state stability.
